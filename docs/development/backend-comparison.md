## Decision (plan 2.3): interp kept, codegen deleted

Measured on 84 corpus cases (178 case x mode rows, every run verified identical to legacy and
across variants), engine 9484204 + worktree. Details in the appendix.

- **Codegen never paid for itself.** On native run + check time alone, the per-row ratio
  interp/codegen-O1 has a median of 0.87-0.95x (codegen slower) and a p90 of 0.98-1.01x in every
  mode and worker count. Only 7 of 525 rows were more than 5% faster (best 1.33x, on a 7 ms
  run). O3 was no better (median 0.88-0.95x).
- **Why.** The interpreter spends 300-5000 ns per executed warp instruction on the corpus. The
  cost is in the shared handlers, and codegen calls those same handlers; dispatch is about 48 ns
  (criterion `interp`). Even on criterion's synthetic register loop, codegen O1/O3 ran 785 / 755 us
  against interp's 755 us.
- **Cost.** A cache hit still cost about 117 ms per call (cargo freshness check, `rustc -vV`,
  dlopen), 3.5-7x a whole interp call. Cold builds took a median of 45 s / 35 s (O1 / O3) per
  kernel and up to 28-34 min. Two kernels did not build within 2 h. Any lowering change misses
  the cache, so this lands in the dev loop.
- **Interp vs legacy** on the whole corpus at that engine: 2.3-2.9x faster by geomean in every
  mode. The scaling gap on large kernels it showed is re-measured at 6ba4190 below.
